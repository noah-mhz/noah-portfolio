Voyagaer

Voyagaer engineers water, wastewater and fuel systems for marine and industrial applications, and supports them across their whole lifecycle. The site is for its customers, new and existing: somewhere to find the right system, read its full specifications and get in touch about it.

    The approach

    I stripped the design back. It borrows the restraint of a premium automotive brand (thin lowercase headings, generous space, one deep sea teal) and pairs it with the depth engineers expect: line drawings instead of renders, full specification sheets and clear routes to an enquiry.

    Minimal, so
    the products lead

    After trying a few directions.
    01 · Drawings, not renders

    One consistent range.

    Every product is shown as a technical line drawing, so the range reads consistently side by side.

    02 · Depth on demand

    As deep as needed.

    Product pages open simply, then go as deep as needed: process, compliance and the full tech sheet.

    03 · Slow motion

    Nothing gimmicky.

    GSAP clip-path wipes and paced scroll reveals, with fast, gimmicky hover effects removed.

    The proudest
    part

    Every spec, one page.

    The product pages. Each one holds all of a system's specs and functions: it opens on deep teal with its own drawing and a one-line promise, then tabs through process and features, compliance and technical specifications, each tab with a layout suited to what it holds.

    The hard
    part

    Content and flow.

    Information and photos arrived in pieces, so I was planning how the site should tell its story, and how customers should move from the company to a product to an enquiry, before I had everything in hand. I tried several flows and layouts before settling on a minimal, professional one.

    My role

    Design system and build.

    A shared design-token system for type, colour and spacing, every section a custom HTML/CSS/JavaScript widget in Elementor, and GSAP/ScrollTrigger motion.

    In the build

    Scoped widgets.

    Many independently built widgets share each page, so every widget's JavaScript is scoped to its own section and they all draw on the same design tokens.

    Next time

    Narrative first.

    I'd settle the flow and the narrative of the site first, and only then start on the design.
